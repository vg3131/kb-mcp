Worx+ Mechanical Polish — Full Process Guide
This is the recommended step-by-step process using Worx+ tooling and chemistry. Product links open directly in the Worx+ catalogue. Hover any step for details. Always run a test patch on a new slab before committing to the full process.
Phase 1 — Surface Grinding
1
#30 Grit — Single Segments
Expose aggregate and establish the floor profile. Mist a little water in front of the machine to keep diamonds cool. Use Cut Sikk on burnished or hard-capped slabs where water alone isn't enough.
💡 Hard slab tip: If diamonds aren't cutting, apply Cut Sikk via backpack sprayer directly in front of the machine. Hard bond diamonds + Cut Sikk = no need for soft bonds on burnished surfaces.
↗ 30 Grit Singles ↗ Cut Sikk
2
#50 Grit — Soft Bond
Remove 30-grit scratch pattern and refine aggregate exposure. Mist water in front of the machine to aid production rate and diamond longevity.
↗ 50 Grit Softs
Phase 2 — Grouting
3
GRT Grouting — Double Segments (120/140/160 Grit)
Wet floor in front of grinder — no puddles. Apply Totally Restore onto floor and broom out for uniform coverage. Push grinder through at a steady, slow pace while product is wet to fill all pores and voids. The GRT simultaneously grinds and works the grout into the surface.
💡 Process note: Keep broom movement side-to-side ahead of the machine. Puddles = product waste and poor penetration. Uniform thin coat = maximum pore fill.
↗ Totally Restore ↗ GRT Segments
Phase 3 — Densification
4
Totally Boost Potassium — First Full Application
After grouting, apply Totally Boost Potassium and keep surface WET for 20–30 minutes. Re-apply in areas that dry fast. Entire area must look darker in colour when dry. Allow to cure and harden for 2–4 hours before proceeding.
💡 Saturation check: The floor must be consistently darker across the whole area before you move on. Any light patches = insufficient densification. Potassium is heavier — it works at this early open-pore stage. Do not rush the curing window.
↗ Totally Boost (K)
Phase 4 — Resin Polishing
5
#50 Specialty Resin — With Potassium Assist
Spray a fine mist of Totally Boost Potassium 5–10m ahead of the machine. Commence with #50 Specialty resin while product is still wet. The wet potassium cools the tool and increases cutting speed. Ideal speed: 700–1000 RPM.
↗ #50 Specialty Resin ↗ Totally Boost (K)
6
Lick Coat — Totally Boost Potassium
Apply a lick coat of potassium densifier between resin stages. Use a microfibre mop if unsure on application amount — this keeps it thin and controlled.
↗ Totally Boost (K)
7
#100 Specialty Resin — Fine Water Mist
Spray an even finer mist than with #50s to keep the tool cool. The floor surface is now denser — less water needed, finer control required.
↗ #100 Specialty Resin
8
Lick Coat — Totally Boost Potassium (Flat Microfibre)
Apply another lick coat of potassium densifier. Use a flat microfibre to control application thickness — this stage requires precision as the surface is closing.
↗ Totally Boost (K)
9
#200 Specialty Resin — Completely Dry
Run this step completely dry. The surface is now dense enough that water would hinder rather than help. No misting.
↗ #200 Specialty Resin
10
Lick Coat — Totally Boost Lithium (Microfibre)
Switch to Lithium densifier at this stage. Lithium has a smaller molecule — it penetrates closed, dense surfaces where potassium can no longer enter. Apply lick coat via microfibre.
💡 Why switch to Lithium: Potassium is too large to penetrate a fully densified surface. Lithium's smaller molecule finds the remaining micro-pores. Applying potassium at this stage is wasted product.
↗ Totally Boost (Li)
11
#400 Universal — Dry  |  Stop Here for Mid Gloss
Dry. Stop here for a mid-gloss finish. Clean the floor with a dry microfibre mop before applying sealer.
↗ #400 Universal
12
#800 → #1500 Flex → #3000 Flex — All Dry
Continue dry for high gloss. #1500 Flex and #3000 Flex for maximum clarity and reflectivity. Stop at #3000 for high gloss. Clean with dry microfibre before sealing.
↗ Flex Pads
Phase 5 — Sealing
13
Totally Defend Impregnating Sealer — 1–2 Coats
Clean floor with dry microfibre first. Shake sealer well before opening. Do not use cold sealer — solvents separate from solids in cool climates. Warm the tin in the sun or a bucket of warm water to reactivate the molecules. Apply 1–2 very light coats via microfibre mop. Once dry (approx. 30–40 min), buff the entire floor with a white or blue pad using a burnisher or polivac.
⚠️ Cold weather warning: If sealer is cold when applied it will not penetrate correctly and will bloom (white haze) on the surface. Always check product temp before opening. See troubleshooting tab for sealer failure restoration methods.
↗ Totally Defend
Gloss level guide:   Mid gloss = stop at #400  ·  High gloss = #400 → #800 → #1500 → #3000  ·  Always clean floor with dry microfibre before sealing  ·  Always test patch on new slabs

22.3 kW satellite motor power — the same power in both the GK-700 and GK-850. The 850 uses its larger footprint and additional disc slots to spread that power across more tooling, managing heat at the tool level. The 700 concentrates it into a narrower footprint for higher point pressure per segment.
Split Chassis — Dynamic Weight Control
The GK's split chassis allows real-time adjustment of head weight while the machine is running. Tipping the chassis back reduces load on the head (useful at startup with aggressive tooling). Pushing it forward increases down pressure. This replaces the fixed weight kit approach — you adjust on the fly without stopping.
"I've been running minimal segments, keeping the chassis back at around #8 on startup. Once we get up to speed I just keep increasing the weight onto the head by shifting the chassis forward."
Active Dual-Motor Planetary System
The GK series uses an independent planetary motor (1.8 kW) driving the planetary head separately from the satellite disc motor (22.3 kW). This is the same two-motor technology that Hayden developed from the King Concepts 820 — the innovation that separated the ProGrind/Worx+ lineage from HTC's single-motor belt-driven system.
"Two motor technology was first seen on a King Concepts machine. That allows us to drive the planetary independently of the main disc motor."
Flat Belt Drive System
The GK uses flat belts rather than poly-V (micro-V) belts. Flat belts handle higher heat ratings, have better minimum wrap diameter for tight pulley configurations, and provide excellent grip. The first 5 King Concepts machines used poly-V belts — Tim and Hayden switched to flat belts to solve tracking issues and heat problems that appeared under sustained high-load grinding.
"The very first five machines we manufactured in King Concepts had poly-V belts. The flat belt solved the tracking issue and handles the heat better."
Wheel Calibration System
The GK features a wheel calibration function accessible through the machine's onboard menu. When the drive hubs are disconnected and need re-locking, the calibration mode allows the operator to independently align each wheel's pins to the hub holes with the machine on the ground — eliminating the need to lift 640–695 kg of machine to align the drive system.
"Go into wheel calibration in the menu. Check left wheel, check right wheel — independent of each other. Give it a little twist and in they go."
GK-850: More Slots, More Tool Options
The GK-850 has additional tool slots per disc versus the GK-700. This is deliberate — a heavier machine can either increase weight per tool (more aggressive) or spread the same weight across more tools (manage heat, extend tool life). The 850 is designed to run at maximum capacity without heat limiting productivity.
"The 850 has a few more slots. You can spread the weight right back within the tolerance zone of the tool so it can continue to perform at maximum capacity."
Transport Mode — Three Positions
GK-700 in transport mode: 160 × 70 × 104 cm (folded). GK-850: 162.5 × 85 × 116.5 cm. Handlebar-extended transport for loading: GK-700 = 219 cm length, GK-850 = 224 cm. Three positions — upright (operating), transport (folded), and handlebar-extended (loading/unloading) — each with specific dimensions for van and trailer planning.
"For those wondering about getting it into a van — you tip the handles, it drops into transport mode, it fits in a standard trailer footprint."
Current Draw as Your Performance Indicator
The GK's display shows live amp draw from both the planetary and satellite motors. This is your real-time performance monitor: increasing chassis weight = more amps. Dull diamonds = lower amps than expected. Hard concrete section = higher amps. Watching current draw tells you more about what the machine is doing than any other single indicator.
"You can see that it's working harder because of the current draw. You know that's coming from the extra weight. The same is true for the opposite."

Startup Sequence
1
Position chassis at #7–#8 (reduced weight) before starting
With aggressive or high-segment-count tooling, starting at full weight puts maximum current demand on the satellite motors at the moment they need the most torque. Set the chassis back to position #7 or #8 to take significant load off the head on startup. Once the machine reaches operating speed, increase weight progressively.
💡 Watch current draw on startup. If amps spike hard and recovery is slow, start at a higher position number (less weight) next time.
2
Select tooling based on concrete and task
For initial shakedowns and standard grinding, start with soft 30 single ROX segments — the same tooling used to benchmark every machine in the grinder series. Singles = higher point pressure, maximum aggression. Doubles = spread load, manage heat. The GK-850's extra disc slots allow more tools per disc — use this to control heat on extended runs.
3
Increase chassis weight progressively once at speed
Once the machine is up to operating RPM and stable, shift the chassis forward toward position #0 to increase head weight. Move one or two positions at a time and monitor amps. Find the position where current draw is at the top of the comfortable operating range without thermal limiting.
4
Use chassis position to navigate hard/soft spots
When approaching a ridge, hard patch, or joint, back off the chassis position (increase number) to reduce load. Glide over the transition point without tool damage. Once past, increase weight again. This real-time adjustment replaces the need to stop, change weight kits, or detour around floor anomalies.
💡 Also increase satellite RPM when backing off weight — the combination of less pressure and more RPM chops down ridges cleanly.
5
Monitor vacuum capacity at GK productivity levels
The GK generates dust significantly faster than the 680/820 class machines. Your 55T vacuum must be operating at full suction capacity — clean filters, correct bag fill rate. At GK productivity levels, a vacuum that was adequate for a 680 may become the bottleneck. Consider the Airworx SCS cyclone separator to prevent vacuum saturation mid-run.
6
Wheel calibration after hub disconnection
If drive hubs have been disconnected (e.g. for transport or service), use the onboard wheel calibration menu to re-lock them. Select left or right wheel independently. The machine shows you alignment status — give the wheel a slight manual rotation until the pins align, then lock. Do not attempt to lift the machine to align by spinning wheels.

The GK series is the direct descendent of the machine Tim Van der Veen built in 1997 — the original King Concepts 820 was the first commercial concrete grinder with a two-motor active planetary system. Every iteration since has built on that core architecture.
1990s
HTC 800 — The Starting Point
The HTC OG 800 was the original large-format concrete grinder used in Australia. Single motor, belt-driven secondary (the "elastic band around the top") to control planetary rotation. Very impressive for its time. Tim Van der Veen flew to HTC Sweden to request reliability improvements — HTC had already sold distribution rights to a competitor. The response: "Gloves are off."
1997
King Concepts 820 — Two-Motor Innovation
Tim invented dual-drive and the active planetary system. 20mm wider than the HTC 800. The King Concepts 820 had an additional satellite motor driving the planetary independently — a second controller, more brains, more cost, but transformative performance. Tim applied for a government R&D grant, filed the patent, and the King Concepts lineage began. First 5 machines used poly-V belts; switched to flat belts after heat and tracking issues emerged.
2000s
ProGrind 680 RC — Australia's Favourite Machine
The 680 RC became the most widely adopted concrete grinder in Australia. Narrower footprint than the 820, same core electrical architecture. The "Goldilocks machine" — fits everywhere, does everything. Still in active use by thousands of contractors. The 680 Big Block variant added more weight for harder surfaces while retaining the same drivetrain.
2010s
ProGrind 830 — More Power, New Electronics
The 830 introduced more horsepower and new-generation electronic drives — a genuine upgrade over the 680 class. The 690 was also introduced during this era with the same horsepower as the 680 but heavier construction and larger discs — underpowered for its weight on Australian 415V. The 830 remains the premium pre-GK machine in the Worx+ range.
2019–24
GK Development — 6 Years of R&D
Development of the King Series began early in Hayden McDonald's tenure at Worx+. Assembly happened weeks before the World of Concrete 2025 deadline — final fabrication vendors (laser cutters, powder coaters, zinc platers, engineers) all shut down for Christmas break with the machines still in parts. The R&D team made it to Las Vegas with working prototypes. Post-reveal, the machines were returned to Australia for final tuning tweaks before contractor release.
Jan 2025
World of Concrete 2025 — Public Launch Current
GK-700 and GK-850 revealed at World of Concrete, Las Vegas. Four machines on the Worx+ booth. Josh described the response as "overwhelming." The machines ran for multiple days on the WoC floor — first true public operational test. The reveal was covered in TechTalk Ep. 25 (World of Concrete Recap) and Ep. 26 (Building The King Series Grinders). First contractor field deployments began immediately post-event.

Key Features Explained
⚡ 22.3 kW Permanent Magnet Satellite Motor
A completely new 22 kW permanent magnet motor — far more efficient and powerful than previous-generation induction motors. Permanent magnet motors deliver full torque from zero RPM, meaning no sluggish startup and more consistent power delivery throughout the grinding run. Hayden described it as a significant leap over the motors used in previous King Concepts and RC machines.
The 22 kW motor is why the GK700 left the first shakedown operator "shell shocked" — cutting deeper on the first 15-minute run than expected with soft 30 singles.
⚙ Split Chassis — Variable Head Weight
The split chassis is the most operationally significant innovation on the GK series. By shifting the chassis position forward or back, the operator changes how much weight sits on the grinding head — from 205 kg (position #10) up to 327 kg (handlebar forward) on the GK-700. This is done while the machine is running, in real time. Use it to: ease startup torque with aggressive tooling, glide over ridges without tool damage, match weight to concrete hardness on the fly.
"I've been keeping the chassis back at around about 8, which takes a significant amount of load off the head on startup. Then once we get up to speed I just keep on increasing the weight by shifting the chassis forward." — Josh Van der Veen
○ Three-Head Configuration — Why Not Four?
The GK series is a three-head machine by deliberate design, not limitation. Three heads deliver better floor coverage geometry and eliminate the directional bias inherent in four-head planetary machines. The three-head setup combined with the dual-drive system (independent satellite and planetary motors) gives the operator more control over how the machine cuts. Four-head machines cover 67% of the floor; three heads at correct overlap cover more efficiently.
The 850 has more tool slots per disc than the 700, allowing more segments to be spread across the larger disc — managing heat while maintaining maximum weight and productivity.
📱 Smart Remote Control System
The GK series uses a next-generation smart remote control transmitter. Unlike the simple on/off remotes of earlier RC machines, the GK remote gives full variable speed control, direction, satellite RPM adjustment, and chassis position — all from the transmitter. The electronics module between the remote and the machine interprets and manages the operator's inputs, creating a machine that responds as an extension of the operator rather than as a separate device.
The remote control frees the operator entirely from pushing the machine — enabling simultaneous management of vacuum, tooling changes, densifier application and quality checking while the machine is running.
🔧 Wheel Calibration Feature
When the hub locks are disconnected and need to be re-engaged, the GK's wheel calibration feature in the control menu aligns the pins and hubs electronically — without lifting the machine. On a 640–695 kg machine, this is essential. Go to wheel calibration in the menu, select left or right wheel independently, and the machine aligns the hub to the locking position. Takes seconds instead of requiring multiple people to lift and spin the wheel.
Wheel calibration can be done independently for left and right wheels — useful when only one hub has come out of alignment.
💰 Flat Belt Drive — Why Not Poly-V?
The GK uses flat belts (not poly-V or micro-V belts) in the head drivetrain. Flat belts handle higher operating temperatures and tighter wrap diameters than poly-V belts — critical in a grinding head that generates significant heat. Poly-V belts were used in the first five King Concepts machines and created tracking problems. Flat belts eliminate this. The first five machines are the prototype story of why flat belts won.
Poly-V belts were trialled in early King Concepts prototypes. Tracking issues under load led the team to flat belts. The GK carries that lesson from the first machine ever built.
Operating Guide — Getting the Best From Your GK
1
First run — start with soft 30 singles
Every GK shakedown at Works Plus starts with soft 30 single segments from the ROX range. If the concrete is too hard for soft 30s, switch tooling — but always start here to baseline the machine. Soft 30 singles deliver maximum point pressure for first cut on any concrete type.
2
Startup with aggressive tooling — tip the chassis back
When loading aggressive low-segment tooling (high point pressure = high startup torque demand), keep the chassis at position 8–10 on startup. This significantly reduces load on the head. Once the machine reaches running speed, progressively shift the chassis forward to increase working weight. Watch current draw — this is your real-time load indicator.
3
Use current draw to read the floor
The GK's control panel shows real-time current draw. Higher current = more load on the machine. Use this to understand what's happening on the floor. If current spikes on a soft patch, shift the chassis back. If current drops, shift forward to add weight. This gives you an objective measure of productivity and tool loading across the entire job.
4
GK-850 — more tool slots = better heat management
The 850 has more tool slots per disc than the 700. Use these slots. Running more segments spreads the machine weight across more diamond contact area, reducing per-segment heat load. The 850 can carry full weight and maximum tool count simultaneously without overheating — this is where its real productivity advantage lies over the 700.
5
Use the chassis to handle ridges and floor inconsistencies
On any floor with ridges, joints or soft spots: shift the chassis back to reduce weight before approaching the ridge, glide over it, then shift forward again on the other side. This protects tooling from impact damage and prevents the machine from digging into soft areas. It takes one hand on the chassis — it becomes second nature within an hour on the machine.
6
Wheel calibration after any hub disconnection
If a hub has been disconnected (intentionally or not), use the Wheel Calibration function in the machine menu before starting. Select left or right wheel independently, align the pins to the hub holes (a gentle twist of the wheel is usually all that is needed), lock in, then proceed. Never attempt to align by lifting the machine — 640+ kg requires the calibration system.
