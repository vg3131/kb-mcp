# Totally Defend (Solvent vs Water Based)

Totally Defend impregnating sealers come in both solvent-based and water-based formulations. Both perform excellently on mechanically polished concrete, stone and terrazzo. The objective: maintain spills at the top surface level, allowing cleaning without permanent damage. Neither is a topical barrier — they work from inside the concrete to protect it.

## Tag

Sealer · Impregnating

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess conditions

Check ambient temperature, surface temperature and relative humidity. Both variants need 15–25°C and below 75–80% RH. If there are known moisture issues in the slab or high ambient humidity, choose the water-based formulation — it is significantly less sensitive to moisture than the solvent-based product.

### Step 2: Warm the product (solvent)

In cool conditions, solvents separate from solids inside the tin. The product looks normal but will not penetrate correctly. Warm the tin in the sun or a bucket of warm water before opening — this reactivates the molecules and restores correct viscosity.

### Step 3: Clean the floor

Sweep and dry mop the floor thoroughly. Remove any polishing residue, dust or contamination. Any material between the sealer and the concrete will affect penetration.

### Step 4: Apply in thin, even coats

Apply via microfibre mop in light, even coats — 1 to 2 coats maximum. Thin application is critical. Over-application creates a surface film that cannot cure correctly and will haze.

### Step 5: Buff within the application window

Once dry (approx. 30–40 minutes), buff the entire floor with a white or blue pad on a burnisher or polivac. Buffing is not optional — it drives the sealer in and removes any excess that remains on the surface.

## Field Tips

- Solvent-based reapplication cycle: 5–8 years. Water-based: 3–5 years. Choose based on project conditions and client expectations.
- An impregnating sealer limits damage to the top surface only — if a stain occurs, a technician can re-polish just the affected area and re-apply sealer without replacing the floor.
- If the sealer hazes (blooms), the surface was too cold, too wet, or too much product was applied. See the Sealer Failure restoration methods in the Troubleshooting section.
